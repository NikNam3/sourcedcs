RSU list mockup, revision 1, 2026-10-14 (ADR 0120, for approval)

The Runway Supervisory Unit works VFR training traffic in Incirlik's pattern by eye, on its own frequency (H130, H138). Its only Bay is this list: one row per aircraft it has, with pattern or straight-in and touch-and-go, full stop or low approach, and the remarks. No legs. AP hands an aircraft over only after the pilot has cancelled IFR. Callsigns, remarks and the RSU frequency are invented.

The columns

Callsign (and 2× for a formation until it splits), type, PAT/STR (pattern or straight-in), T&G/FS/LA (touch-and-go, full stop, low approach), remarks (cut at a word, click to read or edit), and the one action due. Empty choices show as –; AP may set them before the handoff when the pilot asks for them.

How a row comes and goes

Comes: AP's handoff (offered → Accept; then Checked in when the pilot calls; control moves then, ADR 0122). Goes: Landed on a full stop (the Strip goes to GC, Taxi in), Breakout or Leaves pattern (the Strip ends; the pilot calls APP). A touch-and-go or low approach that stays keeps its row: set the next option.

Left out on purpose

Legs (closed, initial, break, base, final), time in the pattern, beacon code, approach count, "long in pattern" and "crowded final" advisories. The RSU sees the aircraft; the list only holds what it cannot see: who it has and what each one asked for.
