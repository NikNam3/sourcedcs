AIRSP-2P-B: outlines, routes and the airspace layer menu, for approval

A delta on the approved airspace panel mockup (AIRSP-1, docs/wip/AIRSP-mockup.html): only what that mockup did not show. That is the OUTLINE section of the editor for an AREA (draw on the map or copy a DCS drawing), the ROUTE editor for an MTR (fixes, legs, widths, the derived envelope), the map while drawing, and the airspace layer menu (H100 DA-9) with its default of ACTIVE only. Everything else in the editor (ID, name, type, frequencies, Save, conflicts, history) is unchanged from the approved mockup. The colour rule is the same (ADR 0056/0058): saved outlines are grey, the outline being edited is the blue accent, and orange-red appears only when something is wrong. Theater: Syria (transition altitude 10,000 ft, so FL from 10,000 up). All airspaces, fixes, navpoints and times are invented sample data. Design notes and open questions: docs/wip/AIRSP-2P-B.md.

The OUTLINE section is new and sits under the approved field grid; for an AREA it replaces nothing. The source chip says where the points came from (NONE, DRAWN, DCS DRAWING). The picker lists the loaded mission's drawings grouped by DCS layer, with the shape and the point count the outline will get: a circle or oval becomes 64 points, a rectangle 4. One section, six states. Every disabled button is the dashed outline with its reason written under it (ADR 0056), never a silent grey. A changed outline gets the same dot and grey "was" line as a changed field. Nothing is saved until Save: drawing, picking, refreshing and clearing only fill the form, and Cancel undoes them. The thumbnail is a north-up sketch of the points; blue marks the points in the form that are not saved yet. State (b) is found by comparing the copied points with the drawing in the loaded mission, so it needs no stored hash. State (f) is the R3-23 lock 2P-A already applies to the other fields.

For kind: ROUTE the OUTLINE section is replaced by the ROUTE section (S-R3-80). One row per fix; the leg columns on a row describe the leg from that fix to the next, so the last row is the exit fix and has none (ADR 0098 §5). A fix name is free text (a letter or a navpoint name; there is no fix database, H23). Coordinates are typed (degrees and decimal minutes shown; decimal degrees and DMS paste accepted, Q2), picked from the mission's navpoints, or placed on the map with ✛. Width is the total corridor width, as the ADR stores it; the "±2 each side" hint is there because MTR charts quote width either side. ALT LOWER/UPPER become read-only and derived for a route. Rows reorder by drag on the number. The same R3-23 lock applies: on an ACTIVE route the whole section is read-only with the reason. The card's activation labels (Confirm, Delay) are 2P-A's and are not shown here.

4 · The map while drawing an outline

DRAWING OUTLINE · MOA ADANA SOUTH · 4 points Click to add a point. Click the first point, or press Enter, to finish. Backspace removes the last point. Esc cancels and leaves the form as it was.

"Draw on map" puts the scope map into a drawing mode; the editor stays open in the panel. The outline being drawn is the blue accent (selection, not a state) with a filled tint; the rubber band runs to the cursor and back to the first point, whose handle is heavier because clicking it closes the shape. The cursor readout gives the coordinate and the length of the leg being drawn. Saved airspaces stay visible in grey, as the layer menu has them, so a new outline can be drawn against its neighbours. Finish hands the points to the form, which shows "DRAWN · 5 points" and the change dot; nothing is sent until Save.

5 · Editing vertices, with a crossing edge

EDITING OUTLINE · MOA ADANA SOUTH · 5 points Drag a point to move it. Drag a small midpoint to insert a point. Select a point and press Delete to remove it (not below 3). Edges cross at 2 places. Move the selected point back, or Done is disabled.

"Edit on map" (or Finish, then a click on the outline) shows a handle on every point and a dashed midpoint handle on every edge. The selected point is filled; its old place is a faint ghost while dragging. A self-intersection is checked by 2P-A's validator as the point moves: the crossing edges and the crossing points turn orange-red (something is wrong), and Done is disabled with the reason, the same rule as Save. The 200-point limit counts here too.

6 · Adding route fixes on the map, snapping to a navpoint

ADDING FIXES · VR1207 · 3 fixes Click to add a fix at the end. Near a navpoint the click snaps to it and takes its name. Enter or Finish returns to the table.

The same drawing mode for a ROUTE adds fixes in order. The corridor is drawn as it will be shown, buffered by each leg's width (the second leg is 10 NM wide, the others the 4 NM default), so a wrong width is visible at once. Navpoints (missionData.waypoints) are the grey triangles; within a few pixels a click snaps to one and takes its name. Altitudes are typed in the table afterwards: the map only places points.

The default reproduces "outline the ACTIVE airspaces" (DA-9 a) without making it a rule. Every ACTIVE airspace is a solid grey outline with a two-line label: name, then limits (FL from the transition altitude up). A route is its corridor, buffered by the leg widths. Nothing is coloured. The menu opens from the small "Airspaces" button on the map and is also mounted in the SETTINGS panel. Each airspace has a three-way switch: Filter (follow the kind and state boxes), On or Off (wins over the boxes). The grey word on the right says what that airspace does right now, so the rule never has to be worked out. An airspace with no outline is listed but cannot be drawn.

A non-ACTIVE airspace is a dashed, lighter grey outline whose label carries its state (MOA ADANA SOUTH · RETURNED, shown through the state filter; W-21 KARATAS · SCHEDULED, shown by its own On switch although SCHEDULED is off). What this does not show

  • The rest of the editor, the card actions, conflicts, history and delete: unchanged from the approved mockup (2P-A).
  • The MTR note on the Strip ("route active" / "route not active" in grey in the MTR group): one read-only line, no layout change.
  • The map label placement algorithm: labels sit at the outline's centroid, or beside the first leg for a route.
  • A route picked from a DCS line drawing (open question Q3).
