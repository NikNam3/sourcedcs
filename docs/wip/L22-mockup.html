1 · APP scope, ATC-only session

Every track here is scheme: 'ATC' because you hold only an ATC Position (H50). Relation to you picks the block: FDB (white = yours; yellow = pointed out to you), PDB (green, someone else's), LDB (green, nobody's), or none (primary only). The "ATC map background" toggle in the bar redraws this scope live. Scene 4 shows the result with it off.

    2 · Handoff and point-out, both ends

    Each step shows the sender's scope and the receiver's scope side by side. The steps play automatically every 3.5 s. Click a step to hold it. Blinks and click-downs are local UI state on each scope, never synced, as on STARS. Handoffs with a pending state exist only between APP and CTR (guide §8). A same-Facility drag, such as TWR→APP, is immediate and shows only the post-accept blink.

    3 · Mixed session: TAC_C2 + APP

    You hold TAC_C2 (it sees the E-3's radar and the datalink) and APP (the LTAG approach radar). Per H50, each track is drawn by the scheme of the radars that currently see it. Where a tactical radar sees it, the tactical scheme wins, with today's IFF colour, diamond or triangle, and Roboto block. A track only the approach radar sees is drawn STARS. Tactical tracks keep the basemap, so the ATC map background setting is ignored in a mixed session (see question 1).

        Design choices

        These are the build rules the mockup follows. The approved decisions are cited, and the rest are defaults this lane took. Anything with an open question is marked Q.

        • One scheme per track. crc-sync sends scheme: 'TACTICAL'|'ATC' per contact, per session. It is TACTICAL when a current radar of a tactical type, or the datalink, sees the contact, and it stays so for about 2 of that radar's sweeps after the last tactical return. Otherwise it is ATC. A session holding only tactical Positions gets only TACTICAL, and one holding only ATC Positions gets only ATC (H41 S1, H50). A flip counts as a label revision, so it draws at once.
        • Fused targets only. Every ATC track gets a blue disc (#1E78FF) carrying one position character. The disc is never text (7210 §3-9-1d). A coasting track loses the disc.
        • Position character. Your letter or the owner's letter (T/A/C) comes from new per-Position config. M marks any tactical Position while TOFI is ACTIVE (H48). An unassociated beacon gets *, and primary-only gets + (H41 S5, S7). Q4
        • Block by relation. You get an FDB when the track is yours, when it is being handed to you, when it is pointed out to you, when it has an emergency code, when it is in a CA, or when you clicked its PDB. It is a PDB (alt gs) when it is correlated to someone else's Strip, and an LDB (code / alt) when it is squawking but uncorrelated. Primary-only gets no block. A tag, if set, replaces the code on LDB line 1.
        • Colours by relation, never affiliation. White means yours or being handed to you. Green means everything else. Yellow means pointed out to you, and SA. Red means CA and EM/RF/HJ. iffState is not drawn on ATC tracks. The one exception is a declared hostile (D9).
        • FDB lines. Line 0 carries indicators. Line 1 is the callsign plus PO or the recipient. Line 2 is the Mode C altitude in hundreds of feet (no */L marks and no climb digits), then ground speed in tens of knots, time-shared every 2 s with the type. Line 3 is A### with an ↑/↓ trend toward it, then H### (ADR 0058's assigned values).
        • Line 0 stacks by severity, left to right, each item in its own colour: EM/RF/HJ, then CA, then SA, then the conformance tag (BUST± / ALT↑↓ / HDG###, app colours). Q6
        • Blink at 1 Hz, reusing the app's _pulseBright. Blinking lasts until you acknowledge a handoff to you, a point-out to you, CA, EM or UN. An accepted handoff blinks for about 5 s on the sender's scope, and so does the PO of an accepted point-out. Every colour-coded state also carries text or blinks (7210 §3-9-1b).
        • Hostiles (H47). A contact whose shared declaration is hostile (iffOverride) gets a steady yellow SA on line 0. If it has no block, as with primary-only, it gets a one-line block holding just SA. Nothing else about the track changes. Q2 Q3
        • Coast (H41 S6). This is client-only. A correlated contact that stops arriving is held at its dead-reckoned position for the existing fade window (fadeGraceMs + fade, 20 s, about 2 sweeps). It shows CST in the altitude field and has no disc. Uncorrelated contacts fade as they do today. Q5
        • History: five blue dots per track, newest to oldest (TC-08/15). The trail colour never encodes identity.
        • Kept from ADR 0058 (H41 S10). The conformance tag and the CPA overlay are drawn in the app's orange #FF8A4C and amber #E0A83C on both backgrounds. CA replaces the STCA word on ATC tracks.
        • Gestures. A click on the target acknowledges, and then cycles FDB ↔ PDB. A click on the block opens the track panel, as today. A drag moves the block, as today, with the same geo-anchored offsets and the leader in the block's colour. Q7
        • Map background off. It hides the basemap fill, land and water, and elevation contours. Coastline, runways, airspace lines and range rings are drawn dim gray #8C8C8C. ATC text switches to the strict palette with no halo. It is per user and client-only, and is ignored while any held Position is tactical. Q1
        • Font. Roboto Mono Regular 11 px, and Medium for the position character. MapTiler's glyph endpoint (the one in crc-desktop-scope-style.json) serves both. I checked: it returns the real font and not its Noto Sans fallback.
        • Not drawn in v1 (stars.md §5.4): scratchpads, CWT letters, MSAW LA, PTL, altitude filters, beacon-select, WHO, Quick Look, Dwell, and ships and vehicles beyond a disc with */+.

        What drives each element

        The track wire comes from presentation.js's presentTrack(), which carries only what the session's sensors know (ADR 0059). "EFSP" means the Board snapshot and deltas every client already receives. The client filters that by what it holds, so it has every Strip, including mission lines. "Client" means state the renderer derives itself.
