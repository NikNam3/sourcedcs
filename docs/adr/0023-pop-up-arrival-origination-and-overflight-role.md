# 0023 — `APP` regains `ARRIVAL` origination, and a real `OVERFLIGHT` Strip Role

## Context

Live testing against real ATC scenarios found five this app had no answer for: a VFR aircraft picking up an IFR clearance back to Incirlik airborne; a military aircraft returning from a MOA direct to home base; an aircraft from an uncontrolled field entering Center's airspace with zero flight plan on file, requesting an IFR return; a short local IFR training flight that never really leaves the area before coming home; and an IFR flight to an uncontrolled destination cancelling IFR near the field.

Research against `EFSPImplementationGuide.md` (repo root, not `docs/` — a broader filesystem search after an earlier session missed it) found:

- The domain model is IFR-only — no `flightRules`/VFR field exists anywhere in the FDR/Identity schema (§3.1–3.2); "VFR" never appears in the guide.
- Two of the five scenarios (the VFR pop-up, and the uncontrolled-field arrival) fall under §12's own Deliberate Deferrals table: *"En-route `(flight, fix)` strip posting — Structurally different data model... Do not retrofit; design separately."* Not an oversight — a conscious v1 boundary.
- A third (the MOA return) generalizes through `TOFI`/`RANGES`/airspace-ownership-as-a-direction (§4.6.3–4.6.4), all still fully deferred (`docs/adr/0020`) — untouched by this ADR.
- The fourth (the local training flight) isn't a gap at all — §3.6's turnaround rule (*"an `ARRIVAL` Strip that reaches `DROPPED` and a later `DEPARTURE` Strip for the same airframe are separate Strips referencing separate FDRs"*) already covers it: Drop the outbound `HANDED_OFF` strip, originate a fresh `ARRIVAL` on the way back in.
- The fifth (cancel IFR near an uncontrolled destination) is already correctly terminal — no Facility exists for the destination, so whoever holds the strip just Drops it, same as any other terminus.

What's actually addressable, and what this ADR does: **there was no way to bring a flight into this app's tracked world except through a real departure (`OPS`) or `CTR`'s `ARRIVAL` self-origination stub** (`docs/adr/0014`), and only `CTR` could even do the latter — `APP` had zero `CreateStrip` permission at all, despite guide §4.1's own Position table listing `APP`'s Strip Roles as `all`.

## Decision

**1. `APP` regains `ARRIVAL` origination** (`permission.js`'s `CREATE_ROLE_PERMISSIONS.APP` gains `'ARRIVAL'`, previously emptied by `docs/adr/0014` point 2 — see that ADR's own "Update" section). Not instead of the real `CTR→APP HANDOFF` `docs/adr/0014` built — alongside it. A flight already being tracked at `CTR` still arrives via the real cross-Facility exchange; a pop-up flight with no sending Facility at all (nothing to `HANDOFF` from) is originated directly by whichever Position — `APP` or `CTR` — actually took the radio call.

**2. A real `OVERFLIGHT` Strip Role**, per guide §2 (it's a named Strip Role) and §6.3 (*"Arrival and overflight Blocks 20/21 surface the radar-automation scratchpads"* — the only Overflight-specific text the guide publishes; no fuller field table exists anywhere, so everything else here is `[SOURCE-DEFINED]`, the same discipline `ARRIVAL_BLOCK_MAP`'s own header comment already uses for itself). This is genuinely separate infrastructure, **not itself an answer to the five scenarios above** — all five are actually bound for Incirlik or already correctly terminal; `OVERFLIGHT` is for a flight transiting Center's (or Approach's) airspace without landing or departing at Incirlik at all, which none of the five describe. Built as real, useful general infrastructure regardless, per the user's explicit request after this distinction was raised during planning.

- **State lifecycle** (`nla.js`): `TRANSITING → DROPPED`, the simplest possible two-state shape, mirroring `DEPARTURE`'s own `HANDED_OFF → DROPPED` terminus. No occupancy gating, no `transferTo` — an overflight was never owned by a chain of Positions leading somewhere; whoever originated it works it until it exits, then Drops it.
- **Permissions**: `OVERFLIGHT_STATE_OWNERS = { TRANSITING: ['APP', 'CTR'] }` (mirrors `ARRIVAL_STATE_OWNERS.INBOUND`'s existing shape). `CREATE_ROLE_PERMISSIONS.APP`/`.CTR` both gain `'OVERFLIGHT'`.
- **Block Map** (`block-map.js`, mirrored in `strip-template.js`): reuses existing generic FDR paths rather than adding new fields — `filed.departureAirport`(8)/`filed.destinationAirport`(8B) mean the flight's *real* origin/destination, never Incirlik (an overflight strip only exists because it isn't landing or departing there). No ground/runway/taxi Blocks (8A, 14, 16–18).
- **Facility config**: new `blockVisibility.OVERFLIGHT` on both Facilities; new `app-overflight`/`ctr-overflight` Bays (`impliesState: 'TRANSITING'`).
- **Client**: `efsp-panel.js`'s `_createStripOrigin()` — previously a single fixed `{Position → role, bayId}` pair per acting Position — now resolves `bayId` from a `(Position, role)` pair via a small lookup table, driven by a new role `<select>` shown only when acting as `APP`/`CTR` (hidden for `OPS`, which still only ever originates `DEPARTURE`). Flight-plan lookup (`efsp-flight-plan-lookup.js`) is skipped for both `ARRIVAL` and `OVERFLIGHT` — for `OVERFLIGHT` specifically, a lookup keyed on "filed a plan departing Incirlik" would be actively wrong, not just unhelpful, since the reused fields mean the opposite thing here.
- **Coordination eligibility** (`coordination.js`'s `COORDINATION_ELIGIBLE_STATES`) is deliberately left untouched — `OVERFLIGHT` has no entry, so the Coordinate button never appears on one. Nothing in the five scenarios requires cross-Facility coordination for overflight traffic; a reasonable follow-on, not bundled here.

## Bug found and fixed along the way

`board-store.js`'s `_applyCreateStrip` derived a new Strip's initial state with a raw `role === 'ARRIVAL' ? 'INBOUND' : 'PROPOSED'` binary — no caller ever passes `op.initialState` explicitly. Adding `OVERFLIGHT` as a third role would have silently landed every new overflight Strip in `'PROPOSED'`, a `DEPARTURE`-only state not even in `OVERFLIGHT_STATE_SET`, with nothing catching it (this assignment doesn't run through `isValidState`). Replaced with a small `DEFAULT_INITIAL_STATE_BY_ROLE` table (`{DEPARTURE: 'PROPOSED', ARRIVAL: 'INBOUND', OVERFLIGHT: 'TRANSITING'}`) — `DEPARTURE`/`ARRIVAL` behavior is unchanged, `OVERFLIGHT` now defaults correctly.

A second bug, also found in live testing (a direct question — "what if someone is doing OPS and APP and CENTER, does the role picker still work?"): `efsp-panel.js`'s `_createStripOrigin()` checked `held.includes('OPS')` first and returned immediately if true, **unconditionally**, never even looking at `APP`/`CTR` — and `_refreshCreateStripAvailability` explicitly hid the role picker whenever `OPS` was held. A controller combining `OPS`+`APP`+`CTR` (entirely plausible under low manning — guide §4.8.1: *"under low manning the combined case is the normal case"*) could only ever originate `DEPARTURE` Strips through this form; `APP`/`CTR` were silently unreachable regardless of what was actually held. This priority-order design predates this ADR (the original `OPS`-then-`CTR` check had the same shape) but only became consequential once `APP`/`CTR` each gained a second role with an actual UI control to pick it. Replaced the priority order with `CREATE_STRIP_ORIGINS`, a flat list of every `(Position, Role)` origin this app can ever originate from, filtered live to whichever are currently held (`_availableCreateStripOrigins()`); the picker now shows every simultaneously-reachable origin (Position included, not just Role) whenever there are 2 or more, and stays hidden (auto-resolving the sole option) for the common single-Position case.

A third bug, introduced by the second fix's own rewrite and caught immediately in the next round of live testing (`PERMISSION_DENIED` on every `CTR` `ARRIVAL` attempt, `actingPositionId: undefined` confirmed via temporary server-side diagnostic logging added and removed for this one investigation): `CREATE_STRIP_ORIGINS`' entries were keyed `positionId`, but `_submitCreateStrip()`'s `sendEfspCreateStrip(origin.actingPositionId, ...)` call — untouched by the rewrite — still read the old field name. Every origin resolved to `actingPositionId: undefined`, which fails `canMutate`'s coarse gate before `canCreateStripRole` is ever reached (`PERMISSIONS[undefined]` is `undefined`). Not a permission-table bug at all — the extensive permission-table re-verification this triggered (direct `require()` checks, full server restarts, ruling out a stale process and a prod-vs-local `crcSyncUrl` mismatch) was chasing the wrong layer entirely; the diagnostic log was what actually found it. Renamed the field to `actingPositionId` throughout `CREATE_STRIP_ORIGINS`/`_availableCreateStripOrigins`/`_createStripOriginKey` instead of touching the consumer, since `actingPositionId` is the name already established everywhere else in this file (e.g. `_resolveActingPositionId`).

## Alternatives considered

- **Only reopen `ARRIVAL` for `APP`, skip `OVERFLIGHT` entirely.** Considered first, since none of the five scenarios actually need `OVERFLIGHT` — all are `ARRIVAL`-shaped. Not what was ultimately built: the user asked for both once the distinction was surfaced, valuing `OVERFLIGHT` as real infrastructure independent of these specific scenarios.
- **Model `OVERFLIGHT` as a variant of `ARRIVAL`** (e.g. a flag on an `ARRIVAL` Strip) rather than a genuine third Strip Role. Rejected: the guide names `OVERFLIGHT` as its own Strip Role (§2), with its own Block Map family (§6.3) — collapsing it into `ARRIVAL` would mean an overflight strip carries `ARRIVAL`-only fields (landing runway, tower hand-off states) that make no sense for a flight that never lands at Incirlik, and would need the same kind of role-conditional logic a real third role gets for free via `BLOCK_MAPS`/`STATES_BY_ROLE`/`STATE_OWNERS_BY_ROLE`'s existing role-keyed shape.
- **Give `OVERFLIGHT` its own dedicated FDR fields** (entry/exit fix, estimated transit times) rather than reusing `DEPARTURE`'s origin/destination/route/altitude/remarks fields. Rejected for this pass — the guide gives no field table to source new fields from, and reuse keeps the diff small; dedicated fields are a reasonable follow-on if `OVERFLIGHT` usage reveals the reused fields are actually confusing in practice.

## Follow-on: "Spawn Return Strip" (data-entry convenience, not a Mutation)

Live testing surfaced the practical cost of §3.6's turnaround rule directly: a controller with a `DEPARTURE` Strip sitting at its `HANDED_OFF` terminus (at `APP` or `CTR`) who needs to originate the *same real flight's* eventual return `ARRIVAL` Strip had to retype every identity field by hand — callsign, aircraft type, wake category, equipment, tail number, unit, home station — since the two Strips share no linkage in the data model at all (by design, per the turnaround rule; not a bug).

Added a "Spawn Return Strip →" button (`bay-view.js`), shown on any `DEPARTURE` Strip at `HANDED_OFF`. First version routed it through the New Strip toolbar — pre-fill the callsign and origin picker, controller reviews and clicks "+ New Strip" themselves. Reworked after direct feedback ("why not just spawn the strip directly?"): there's no actual ambiguity for a review step to resolve here — the callsign and full identity are already known from the source Strip — so the extra click and the toolbar-mediated state (pending-seed guard, origin-picker steering, callsign-field round-trip) bought nothing and was itself extra surface for bugs. `spawnReturnArrivalFrom()` (`efsp-panel.js`) now sends the `CreateStrip` Mutation directly — same "the data's already in hand, skip the toolbar" shape `createStripFromFiledPlan()` already uses for the ops-filed cards' own Create Strip button. Requires actually holding the departure Strip's owning Position (`APP`/`CTR`); the return leg always originates at that same Position/Facility.

**Immediately exposed a deeper, pre-existing gap**: live testing found the copy had nothing to actually copy. `identity.aircraftType`/`wakeCategory`/`tailNumber`/`unit`/`homeStation` had been validated and writable via `fdr-store.js`'s generic `setField()` (all five in `WRITABLE_PATHS` since Phase 1) the entire time, but **no Block anywhere, on any role, client or server, ever routed a `SetBlock` at any of them** — Block 3 only ever displayed them as a read-only composite (`formatBlock3`). Nor did the flight-plan lookup (`toFdrFiledSeed`) ever populate them (it only maps `route`/`altitude`/`departure`/`destination`/`remarks`). The practical result: these five fields have been permanently blank on every Strip this app has ever created, for every role, since before this ADR — not a bug in "Spawn Return Strip," a much older and wider gap it happened to surface.

**Second round of feedback** ("route, departure and arrival aerodrome, squawk, etc all gone") found the *original* filed-flight-plan fields weren't carried over either — only the identity sub-fields from the block-map fix above were. Extended `spawnReturnArrivalFrom()`'s seed: `filed.route`/`requestedAltitude`/`remarks` copy straight across, and `filed.departureAirport` maps onto the new `ARRIVAL` Strip's `originAirport` (Block 8) — the flight's real point of origin doesn't change just because it's airborne; for a sortie that departed Incirlik and never actually landed anywhere else, that departure record's own `departureAirport` *is* the honest answer to "where is this arrival coming from."

Squawk (`identity.beaconAssigned`) needed a different mechanism: `fdr-store.js`'s `createFdr` always auto-allocates a fresh code from the pool — there is no "use this exact code" seed path, by design (the allocator, never the caller, picks it). The only way to carry the same code onto the return leg is a follow-up `SetBlock` once the new Strip actually exists — which is only known from the `CreateStrip` Mutation's own ack (`sendEfspCreateStrip` returns just a `clientMutationId`, not the eventual Strip). Added a small one-shot ack-callback registry (`_onMutationAck`/`_mutationAckCallbacks` in `efsp-panel.js`, fired by `notifyEfspMutationAck`) and extended `applyEfspMutationAck` (`efsp-state.js`) to echo back `strip`/`fdr` in its result so a callback has something to act on. The two Strips end up sharing a beacon code across two now-independent FDRs — expected and accepted, the same way guide defect D23 already treats duplicate codes generally (`code-allocator.js`'s `validateAssignment` returns `{ok:true, warning:'DUPLICATE_IGNORED_WARNING'}` for a code held by a different FDR, never a rejection); the existing warning-surfacing path (`notifyEfspMutationAck`'s `_showMutationWarning`) shows it without any new UI.

Fixed the same way the `docs/adr/0022` `trackDegradationFlag` gap was: added five new Blocks (`'3A'`-`'3E'`, `[SOURCE-DEFINED]`, grouped near Block 3 since they're its constituent identity fields, same numbering convention `'9A-FUEL'` etc. already use), plain `'fdr'`-routed, present on all three roles (`DEPARTURE`/`ARRIVAL`/`OVERFLIGHT`), optional (not required — matches every other identity sub-field precedent). No `fdr-store.js` or `board-store.js` changes needed — the write path was already complete and correct; only the missing Block Map wiring needed closing. Added to the compact Strip view's Block list (`bay-view.js`) so they're actually reachable, not just present in the map with no render path.

## Superseded: "Convert to Arrival" replaces spawning a second Strip entirely

**Third round of feedback** ("rename the button to convert to arrival and just move the departure to the arrival, so never two of the same strips exist") rejected the whole two-Strip design outright, in favor of an in-place role change — deliberately departing from guide §3.6's turnaround rule (*"an `ARRIVAL` Strip that reaches `DROPPED` and a later `DEPARTURE` Strip for the same airframe are separate Strips referencing separate FDRs"*), chosen explicitly for this app's practical needs over strict doctrinal literalism (the same kind of considered deviation `docs/adr/0013`'s shared-`FdrStore` simplification already made elsewhere).

New Mutation kind `ConvertToArrival` (`permission.js`, `board-store.js`). Restricted to `APP`/`CTR` only, the same way the 5 coordination primitives are — split into a new `APP_CTR_ONLY_OP_KINDS` set (not folded into `COORDINATION_OP_KINDS`, since this isn't a cross-Facility exchange or a replica, just a same-Facility in-place role change; it needs the identical exclusion from `OPS`/`CD`/`GND`/`TWR` for an unrelated reason). `board-store.js`'s `_applyConvertToArrival`: validates the Strip is `DEPARTURE` at `HANDED_OFF`, re-checks the role-scoped permission via the *existing* `canCreateStripRole(actingPositionId, 'ARRIVAL')` — deliberately reused rather than a parallel table that would only ever say the same thing — then mutates the Strip in place: `role → ARRIVAL`, `state → INBOUND`, relocated to the owning Position's ARRIVAL Bay (`app-inbound`/`ctr-enroute`), `fdrId` untouched. `annotations`/`flags`/`coordination`/`correlation` all reset to fresh-Strip defaults — none of them mean the same thing under the new role (a `DEPARTURE`-phase annotation note, an already-resolved coordination link, an attention flag from the outbound leg).

This retires the entire spawn-and-copy mechanism above: no `CreateStrip` seed to build, no beacon-code follow-up, no duplicate-code warning, no ack-callback registry (`_onMutationAck`/`_mutationAckCallbacks` removed from `efsp-panel.js`; `applyEfspMutationAck`'s `strip`/`fdr` echo-back reverted in `efsp-state.js`, since nothing needs it anymore). `spawnReturnArrivalFrom()` is replaced by `convertStripToArrival(strip)` — a single `sendEfspMutation(strip.ownerPositionId, strip, {kind:'ConvertToArrival'})` call — and the button is renamed "Convert to Arrival →". There is structurally only ever one Strip and one FDR for this flight throughout its entire lifecycle now; the two-Strip design's whole class of problems (a stale leftover departure Strip, a duplicated beacon code, copying fields by hand) is gone because nothing new is ever created.

**One more bug found immediately in live testing**: converting still lost data — specifically the departure airport. Cause: `DEPARTURE` and `ARRIVAL` read *different* FDR fields for the same underlying fact (Block 8 is `filed.departureAirport` for `DEPARTURE`, `filed.originAirport` for `ARRIVAL` — `block-map.js`). An in-place conversion that never touches the FDR (the whole point — same FDR throughout) left `originAirport` permanently blank even though `departureAirport` still held the right value right next to it, since a `DEPARTURE`-originated FDR never populates `originAirport` at all. Fixed by having `_applyConvertToArrival` remap `filed.departureAirport → filed.originAirport` via the ordinary `setField()` path (no new fdr-store.js method needed) — the flight's real point of origin doesn't change just because it's airborne. `destinationAirport` needs no equivalent remap (`ARRIVAL` has no destination field at all — the Facility itself is the implicit destination). Block 7 (altitude) is **deliberately not remapped** the same way: `DEPARTURE`'s is `filed.requestedAltitude` (what was filed), `ARRIVAL`'s is annotation-routed (guide §3.7's append-only sequence of ATC-*assigned* clearances during descent) — genuinely different concepts, not two names for one fact; seeding it from the filed value would misrepresent a filed altitude as an issued clearance, so leaving it blank for the controller to actively assign is correct, not a gap. Also had to add `fdr: updatedFdr` to `_applyConvertToArrival`'s return value (mirroring `_applySetBlock`'s existing pattern) — without it, `efsp-ws.js`'s ack/broadcast has no FDR to include, and a connected client would never actually see the remapped field at all despite the server-side write succeeding.

## Consequences

- `docs/adr/0014`'s point 2 (empty `APP` `CREATE_ROLE_PERMISSIONS`) is corrected via its own "Update" section rather than rewritten — its point 1 (`CTR`'s `ARRIVAL` right) and points 3–5 (the `HANDOFF`-aware `INBOUND` NLA machinery) are untouched and still fully in effect.
- `TOFI`, `RANGES`, `TACTICAL`, MRU Positions remain fully deferred (`docs/adr/0020`) — the MOA-return scenario's military-boundary half stays unaddressed.
- No VFR/IFR schema distinction was added — the domain model stays IFR-only, matching the guide's own silence on VFR throughout.
- `efsp-permission.test.mjs`, `efsp-block-map.test.mjs`, `efsp-nla.test.mjs`, `efsp-board-store.test.mjs`, `efsp-facility-config.test.mjs` (crc-sync) and `efsp-block-map-parity.test.js`, `efsp-nla-client.test.js` (crc-desktop) all gained `OVERFLIGHT` coverage; several pre-existing tests that literally asserted `OVERFLIGHT`/empty-`APP` as the "unbuilt"/"narrowed" cases were updated to use a still-genuinely-unbuilt role (`MISSION`, guide-named but WP7-deferred) as their example instead.
- `efsp-permission.test.mjs` and `efsp-board-store-coordination.test.mjs` gained `ConvertToArrival` coverage: the in-place role/state/Bay change, the annotations/flags/coordination/correlation reset, rejection outside `DEPARTURE`/`HANDED_OFF`, the `APP`/`CTR`-only restriction (D21-style), and the same behavior at `CTR` after a real cross-Facility `HANDOFF`. One pre-existing test ("every Position may perform every non-CreateStrip, non-coordination op kind") needed updating to also exclude the new `APP_CTR_ONLY_OP_KINDS` category.
- A flight that goes `DEPARTURE → ConvertToArrival → ARRIVAL` and is later dropped, then departs again some other day, still gets a genuinely fresh Strip/FDR at that point (via ordinary `CreateStrip`) — this ADR's departure from §3.6 is scoped narrowly to the same-day return-leg case `ConvertToArrival` handles, not a wholesale abandonment of "one FDR per flight per operating day."
