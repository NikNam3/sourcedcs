# 0010 — NLA transitions (and the equivalent drag path) are gated by per-State authority, not just Strip ownership

## Context

Guide §3.4's EFSP State table has a "normally owned by" column — `PROPOSED`→`OPS`, `PENDING_CLEARANCE`/`CLEARED`→`CD`, `HELD`→`CD`/`GND`, `PUSHBACK`/`TAXI`→`GND`, `RUNWAY_QUEUE`/`LUAW`/`DEPARTED`→`TWR`, `HANDED_OFF`→`APP`. Prior to this ADR, nothing in the codebase enforced it. `board-store.js`'s only authority check for `InvokeNla`, `MoveStrip`, `SetFlag`, `SetBlock`, etc. was raw Strip ownership (`strip.ownerPositionId === actingPositionId`, guide §4.4) — and Strip ownership does not change on a state-only NLA transition, only on an explicit `TransferStrip`.

The consequence, found during manual testing: `OPS` originates a Strip (owns it, `PROPOSED`), presses "Send to Clearance" (a legitimate `PROPOSED`-state action), and the Strip advances to `PENDING_CLEARANCE` — but ownership never moves, because state-only transitions don't transfer ownership. Nothing stopped `OPS` from continuing to press the NLA button and walking the Strip through `PENDING_CLEARANCE → CLEARED → PUSHBACK → ... → DEPARTED`, doing the entire job of `CD`, `GND`, and `TWR` single-handedly, as long as it simply never transferred the Strip away. Guide §4.1 explicitly marks `OPS` as "not a control position" — this let it act as one anyway, for every downstream state, purely by omission.

A related, sharper hazard: the NLA button relabels in place after each press (guide §3.5's own design — one button, one label per State). The existing 400ms double-tap guard only catches a literal double-tap of the *same* action; it does nothing once the label has changed. A second, entirely deliberate-looking click on the same physical spot a second or two later — e.g. a controller double-checking their first click registered — fires a genuinely different, real transition that may belong to a different Position's job entirely.

## Decision

`permission.js` gains a new table, `STATE_OWNERS_BY_ROLE` (`DEPARTURE_STATE_OWNERS` sourced from guide §3.4's table exactly; `ARRIVAL_STATE_OWNERS` is `[SOURCE-DEFINED]`, mirroring the same shape since the guide gives ARRIVAL no equivalent published table), and a function `canActOnState(actingPositionId, role, state)` checking whether the acting Position is authorized to advance a Strip *out of* its current state.

This is checked in **addition to**, never instead of, the existing ownership gate — both must hold. It's enforced at two points in `board-store.js`, deliberately not just one:

1. **`_applyInvokeNla`** — the NLA-button path. Checked after the 400ms double-tap early-return (a genuine double-tap of an already-applied transition doesn't need a fresh authority check) and before `computeNla` runs.
2. **`_validateBayImpliedTransition`** — the drag path (used by both `_applyMoveStrip` and `_applyTransferStrip`). Guide §3.5 rule 4 makes NLA "an accelerator, not the only path" — every transition reachable via the button must also be reachable via drag, which means every permission gate on the button must equally apply to the drag. Gating only `_applyInvokeNla` would leave a trivial bypass: drag the Strip directly into a Bay whose implied state matches what the disallowed NLA press would have produced.

Both call sites reject with `{ok:false, reason:'PERMISSION_DENIED', detail: '<state> is not <actingPositionId>'s to advance'}`.

## Alternatives considered

- **Leave it as a procedural expectation, matching real-world physical possession.** This was the original design (guide §4.4: "ownership eliminates most conflicts by construction... this is why the EFSP uses optimistic concurrency rather than a CRDT"), and it's a defensible reading of the guide's text, which never explicitly mandates a second technical gate beyond ownership. Rejected once the concrete hazard was identified: unlike a real paper strip, which a `CD` controller literally cannot walk across the room and stamp without `OPS` first physically handing it over, nothing here stops a single controller (or a compromised/careless one) from silently doing another Position's entire job. The project owner's judgment was that this needed a hard technical gate, not just convention.
- **Gate only the NLA button.** Rejected: leaves the drag path as a trivial, unguarded bypass — see above.
- **Auto-transfer the Strip as part of the NLA action** (e.g. "Send to Clearance" also relocates the Strip to `CD`). Rejected: this is a materially different, larger design change (automation actively moving a Strip between Positions, which guide §10.3 warns against in the surveillance-auto-advance context and which extends naturally here too), and it would remove the controller's ability to choose *when* and *whether* to hand a Strip off — a real workflow need (a controller may want to advance the state and hold the physical Strip a moment longer). The chosen fix instead makes the *lack* of a hand-off loudly block further progress, forcing the explicit transfer, without deciding *for* the controller when to make it.

## Consequences

- **A hand-off must now actually happen for the lifecycle to proceed.** `OPS` advances `PROPOSED → PENDING_CLEARANCE`, then must explicitly transfer the Strip to `CD` before anyone can press "Mark Cleared" — including a controller who holds both `OPS` and `CD` at once, since `_resolveActingPositionId` (client) always prefers the Strip's *current owner* when resolving which Position to act as, and ownership doesn't move until the transfer happens. This is a genuine behavior change from Phase 1/2's earlier assumption that ownership was cosmetic once a controller held every relevant Position.
- **The transfer itself is cheap and un-gated** when its target Bay's implied state matches the Strip's current state (a same-state relocation, not an advance — e.g. transferring a `PENDING_CLEARANCE` Strip into `cd-pending-clearance`) — `_validateBayImpliedTransition`'s existing early return (`impliedState === strip.state`) already skips the new check entirely in that case, so a controller finishing their part and hand-off in one drag doesn't require two separate authorized actions.
- `efsp-index.test.mjs`'s existing "mid-DEPARTED-to-APP-transfer" restart test needed rewriting to reflect a realistic hand-off chain (`OPS` sends to clearance → transfers to `TWR` once `DEPARTED` → `TWR`, the real owner of `DEPARTED`, presses the real Hand Off) rather than one Position holding the Strip end-to-end — which was itself a symptom of the gap this ADR closes.
- The accidental-relabeled-button hazard is closed as a side effect: a second press past the double-tap window on a Strip that's crossed into another Position's territory is rejected, not silently applied.
- **Follow-up, same session:** the client now also renders this proactively. `efsp-nla.js` gained a mirror of `STATE_OWNERS_BY_ROLE`/`canActOnState` (a UX convenience only — the server check above remains the sole authority), and `bay-view.js`'s NLA button is rendered `disabled` with a distinct greyed style and a `title` tooltip naming the reason when `strip.ownerPositionId` isn't authorized for `strip.state`, rather than only failing after a click reaches the server. This also fully closes the accidental-relabeled-button hazard from the button's side, not just the server's: a disabled `<button>` doesn't dispatch `click` at all, so a second press past the double-tap window can't even reach `_invokeNla`. A test (`efsp-nla-client.test.js`) asserts the client table stays byte-identical to `permission.js`'s server table, since a silent drift here would be actively misleading (a button rendering as pressable when the server would actually reject it) rather than merely cosmetic. This mirror pattern is the same one `NLA_LABELS` already uses for the same reason. The remaining, still-open gap is every `computeNla`-level inhibit (missing beacon code, invalid flight plan, a hold in force, ...) — those still only surface after a click, since mirroring `computeNla` itself client-side would mean duplicating FDR-dependent logic, not just a static table, and wasn't part of this ADR's scope.
